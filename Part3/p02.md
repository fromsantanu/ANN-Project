## **Chapter 11: Preparing the Dataset**

---

### **1. Why Dataset Preparation Matters**

Before we train any model, we must organize our data properly.

👉 Think of it like cooking:

* Good ingredients → good result
* Messy ingredients → bad result

---

### **2. Two Important Parts**

In machine learning, we always divide data into two parts:

👉 **Input (X)** and **Output (y)**

---

### **3. What Is Input (X)?**

Input (X) contains all the **features**:

* Age
* BP
* Sugar
* Weight
* Cholesterol

👉 This is what we *give* to the model

---

### **4. What Is Output (y)?**

Output (y) is the **answer**:

* 0 → No risk
* 1 → High risk

👉 This is what the model tries to learn

---

### **5. Small Dataset Example**

Let’s take a simple dataset:

| Age | BP  | Sugar | Weight | Cholesterol | Risk |
| --- | --- | ----- | ------ | ----------- | ---- |
| 45  | 130 | 85    | 70     | 200         | 0    |
| 60  | 150 | 120   | 80     | 240         | 1    |
| 50  | 140 | 95    | 75     | 210         | 0    |
| 65  | 160 | 130   | 85     | 260         | 1    |

---

### **6. Separating X and y**

From this table:

#### **Input (X)**

```id="k2n8df"
[
 [45, 130, 85, 70, 200],
 [60, 150, 120, 80, 240],
 [50, 140, 95, 75, 210],
 [65, 160, 130, 85, 260]
]
```

---

#### **Output (y)**

```id="m9x3pl"
[0, 1, 0, 1]
```

---

### **7. Understanding This Clearly**

* Each row in X → one patient
* Each value in y → result for that patient

👉 Model learns mapping:

```id="u4p9sa"
X → y
```

---

### **8. Important Note**

* X is usually **2D (rows and columns)**
* y is usually **1D (list of outputs)**

👉 This format is required for training

---

### **9. Real-Life Analogy**

Think of exam preparation:

* Questions → Input (X)
* Answers → Output (y)

👉 Model learns how to go from question → answer

---

### **🎯 Key Idea of This Chapter**

👉 **Dataset = Input (X) + Output (y)**

This is the foundation of training.
