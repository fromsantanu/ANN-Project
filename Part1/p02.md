## **Chapter 2: How Humans Make Decisions**

---

### **1. Start with a Simple Situation**

Think about a doctor again.

A patient comes in, and the doctor observes:

* Age
* Blood Pressure
* Sugar level
* Weight
* Cholesterol

Now, the doctor does **not** look at just one thing.

👉 The decision is made by **considering all factors together**.

---

### **2. Real-Life Thinking Process**

Let’s say:

* BP is slightly high → not too serious
* Sugar is normal → good sign
* Age is high → some risk

Now the doctor mentally combines everything:

👉 “Overall, the risk is moderate.”

This is **not a fixed rule**, but a **combined judgment**.

---

### **3. Another Everyday Example**

Think like this:

You are deciding whether to carry an umbrella:

* Dark clouds ☁️
* Weather forecast says rain 🌧️
* Wind is strong 🌬️

You don’t depend on just one factor.

👉 You combine all signals and decide: *“Yes, I should carry it.”*

---

### **4. What Is Happening Internally?**

Even though we don’t see it, the brain is doing something like this:

* Giving importance (weight) to each factor
* Combining them
* Making a final decision

For example:

* BP might matter more than weight
* Sugar might matter more than age

👉 Some inputs influence the decision **more than others**.

---

### **5. Converting This Idea to Our Problem**

In our health prediction model:

* Inputs = patient data
* Decision = risk prediction

So the system should learn:

👉 How to **combine inputs intelligently** to produce an output.

---

### **6. Simple Representation**

We can think of it like:

```
Decision = (Age influence)
         + (BP influence)
         + (Sugar influence)
         + (Weight influence)
         + (Cholesterol influence)
```

👉 Each input contributes something to the final decision.

---

### **7. Key Insight**

Humans don’t follow strict formulas like:

❌ “If BP > 140 → Risk = 1”

Instead, we think:

✔ “Considering everything together → what is the overall risk?”

---

### **🎯 Key Idea of This Chapter**

👉 **Decision = Combination of Inputs**

This is the most important concept before learning neural networks.
